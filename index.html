01 / The issue

Not everything online is true.

Fake news and misleading content can confuse people, damage reputations, and influence decisions. A post may look convincing and still be missing context, using an old photo, or making a claim without reliable evidence.

01

It spreads quickly

Emotional or shocking posts can get shared before anyone checks whether they are accurate.

02

It can mislead people

False claims can shape opinions and choices, especially when they appear to come from a trusted friend.

03

We all have a role

Checking a source and refusing to forward unverified content helps reduce its reach.

02 / The solution

Use SIFT before you share.

Four small checks can help you decide whether a post deserves your trust. You do not need to be an expert—you just need to pause and investigate.

S

Stop

Pause before reacting or forwarding. Strong emotions are a reason to slow down.

I

Investigate the source

Who published it? Check the account, website, author, and their credibility.

F

Find better coverage

Look for other reliable sources reporting the same claim independently.

T

Trace the original

Find the original quote, image, video, or report. Check its date and context.

Remember: A lot of likes, shares, or comments do not prove that something is true.

04 / Your turn

Make your next click count.

Before sharing a post, check the source, confirm the date, and look for reliable evidence. If you cannot verify it, do not pass it on.

A simple habit can make our online community safer.
